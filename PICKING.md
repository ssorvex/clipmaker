# How to pick the clips

The program finds passages that are loud, fast and quotable. It cannot tell
whether a passage is *interesting*. That judgement is this file.

Edit it freely — it is the one place the rules live, and both a scheduled run
and a person working by hand read it before choosing.

---

## What a clip is

One moment that **starts, develops and finishes** inside 30–60 seconds. A
viewer who sees nothing else should still get a whole thing.

The reliable shape is: a setup, a concrete detail, and a turn.

> Rockstar's researcher met a smuggler *(setup)* who built submarines for the
> cartels *(detail)* and had been kidnapped by them *(turn)*.

Fifty-six seconds, nothing missing, nothing needed.

## What to look for, best first

1. **A story with a turn** — something is one way, then it isn't.
2. **A fact that makes you stop** — a real exploit, a secret, a consequence.
3. **Numbers** — money, counts, hours, records. They travel well and they are
   easy to put in a title.
4. **A strong opinion**, if it is actually argued and not just asserted.

## What to skip, however loud it gets

- Intros, outros, "like and subscribe", channel plugs, stream announcements
- Sponsor reads
- Filler excitement — "I can't believe we're finally here", "I'm so hyped".
  The energy is real, the content is nothing, and the signal scores love it.
- Anything that only makes sense if you watched the previous five minutes
- A moment where the speaker is reading someone else's tweet aloud with no
  comment of their own

## Where to cut

- **Start on the hook.** The first sentence should be able to stand alone.
- Never start mid-sentence, and never on a connective: *However, So, And,
  But anyway, Moving on, Now*. Push the start to the next sentence instead.
- **End on the payoff**, not after it. If the speaker explains the joke
  afterwards, cut before the explanation.
- Leave the sentence whole at both ends. A clip that ends mid-word reads as
  broken, not as a cliffhanger.

## Spread

Take the clips from **different parts of the video**. Three moments from the
same two minutes are one clip cut three ways, and they compete with each
other when posted. Aim for one from the first third, one from the middle, one
from the back.

## Titles

State the surprising thing, plainly, in one line.

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| Rockstar hired a real cartel smuggler to research GTA 6 | INSANE GTA 6 SECRET!! 🤯 |
| You can read a GTA 6 code through the case with a flashlight | You won't BELIEVE this trick |
| GTA 6 made $1.5 billion before anyone played it | GTA 6 is making CRAZY money |

No ALL CAPS, no emoji in the title, no "you won't believe". If the fact is
interesting the plain sentence is the strongest version of it. Keep it under
about 70 characters so it is not cut off.

Write the description as one or two sentences that add what the title left
out — not a restatement of it.

## Sound

Place cues on **structure**, never on a random word.

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| 1.2 s before the hook lands | `riser`, then `impact` on the landing |
| A reveal or a concrete detail | `reveal` |
| Building into a shock | `suspense`, then `impact` |
| A turn — "but actually..." | `record_scratch` |
| A number | `tick_up` into it, `ding` on it |
| The closing line | `boom` |

Four to six cues in a 45-second clip. More machine-guns it. The placer will
move each cue to the nearest gap between words and drop any it cannot fit —
that is expected, not a failure.

## Length

30–60 seconds. Below 30 it feels clipped; above 60 the platforms start
treating it differently. If the moment genuinely needs 70 seconds, it is two
clips or none.
